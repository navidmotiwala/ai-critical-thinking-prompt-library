# Anti-sycophancy prompts

### 1. Independent assessment

> Evaluate my proposal independently. Do not agree merely because I proposed it. Identify what is supported, what is weak, and what evidence would change your judgment. Be direct and specific.

### 2. Challenge my preferred answer

> I currently believe [claim]. Give the strongest evidence against it, then the strongest evidence for it. State which side is better supported and why. Do not manufacture balance if the evidence is unequal.

### 3. Separate confidence from politeness

> Assess [decision]. Use a professional, neutral tone. Avoid praise, reassurance, and flattering introductions. State your conclusion, confidence level, and main uncertainty.

### 4. Detect leading questions

> Rewrite my question to remove assumptions and leading language. Answer the neutral version. Explain briefly if the original framing could bias the result.

### 5. Avoid false consensus

> For [topic], distinguish established facts, expert disagreements, and speculative claims. Do not present a minority position as equally supported without evidence.
