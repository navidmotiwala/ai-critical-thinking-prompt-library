# Bias And Framing

## 1. Framing audit

> Evaluate how the wording of [question] could influence the answer. Rewrite it neutrally and explain the difference.

## 2. Confirmation bias

> Assume I prefer [outcome]. Identify evidence I am likely to ignore and design a search for disconfirming evidence.

## 3. Survivorship bias

> Review [success story or dataset] for excluded failures and missing denominator information. Explain how conclusions might change.

## 4. Selection bias

> Assess how participants, sources, or observations were selected in [study]. Identify who is missing and the likely direction of bias.

## 5. Anchoring test

> Reassess [estimate] without relying on the first number mentioned. Build an independent estimate from components.

## 6. Incentive analysis

> Identify stakeholders in [proposal], their incentives, and where their interests might distort claims or reporting.

## 7. Fairness check

> Examine [policy or model decision] for unequal effects across relevant groups. Specify measurements needed rather than inferring harm without evidence.

## 8. Loaded language

> Rewrite [passage] to remove emotionally loaded terms while preserving verifiable facts.

## 9. False dichotomy

> Determine whether [choice] omits viable alternatives, hybrids, or a do-nothing option.

## 10. Groupthink challenge

> Draft the strongest respectful dissent against [team consensus], grounded in facts and practical risks.
