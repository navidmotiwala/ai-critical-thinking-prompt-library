# Decision And Risk

## 1. Decision matrix

> Compare [options] using weighted criteria, assumptions, tradeoffs, and sensitivity to weights. Do not disguise subjective scores as facts.

## 2. Reversibility

> Classify parts of [decision] as reversible or hard to reverse. Recommend low-cost tests before irreversible commitments.

## 3. Kill criteria

> Define measurable conditions under which [project] should be paused, revised, or stopped.

## 4. Second-order effects

> Identify direct and indirect consequences of [action], including delayed effects and unintended incentives.

## 5. Dependency failure

> Map critical dependencies for [plan]. Explain how each failure propagates and propose fallback actions.

## 6. Risk register

> Build a risk register for [initiative] with likelihood, impact, owner, mitigation, and trigger. Distinguish estimates from observed data.

## 7. Sensitivity analysis

> Identify which three assumptions most influence [forecast]. Show how outcomes change across plausible values.

## 8. Cost of delay

> Estimate the cost of delaying [decision] versus acting with incomplete information. State assumptions and uncertainty.

## 9. Pilot design

> Design the smallest safe pilot for [proposal], with success metrics, guardrails, and stop conditions.

## 10. Postmortem without hindsight bias

> Analyze [failure] using information available at each decision point. Avoid treating the eventual outcome as inevitable.
