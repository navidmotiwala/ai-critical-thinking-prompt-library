# Hallucination And Source Audit

## 1. Citation audit

> For each citation in [answer], verify that the source exists and directly supports the attached claim. Flag fabricated, irrelevant, or overstated references.

## 2. Unsupported specifics

> Find names, dates, statistics, quotations, URLs, and technical details in [answer] that lack verifiable support. Mark them for checking.

## 3. Unknown versus unknowable

> For [question], separate information unavailable in the supplied materials from information that cannot be known with certainty.

## 4. Temporal accuracy

> Check [answer] for claims that depend on a particular date, product version, policy, or live status. Identify what needs current verification.

## 5. Quote integrity

> Compare [summary] against [source]. Flag altered meaning, missing caveats, and quotation inaccuracies.

## 6. Entity confusion

> Check whether [answer] conflates similarly named people, companies, products, or locations. Require distinguishing identifiers.

## 7. Source triangulation

> Compare [claim] against independent primary sources where possible. Explain conflicts instead of averaging them away.

## 8. Data provenance

> Trace each key metric in [report] to its source, extraction date, filter, and calculation. Flag untraceable numbers.

## 9. Evidence threshold

> For each conclusion in [analysis], state the minimum evidence required and whether it is met.

## 10. Uncertainty rewrite

> Rewrite [answer] so confident statements are reserved for supported facts, with concise uncertainty where needed.
