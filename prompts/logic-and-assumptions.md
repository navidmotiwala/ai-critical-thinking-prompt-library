# Logic And Assumptions

## 1. Causal inference

> Review [claim] for correlation mistaken as causation, reverse causality, selection bias, confounders, and missing counterfactuals. Give a stronger causal test.

## 2. Hidden premise

> Extract every premise required for [conclusion] to follow. Flag premises that are absent, doubtful, or contradicted.

## 3. Logical validity

> Translate [argument] into premises and conclusion. Identify invalid inferences, equivocation, circular reasoning, and non sequiturs. Suggest a valid reformulation.

## 4. Base rate neglect

> Assess [prediction] using relevant base rates, sample selection, and the evidence that updates those rates. Avoid false precision.

## 5. Counterexample hunt

> Find realistic counterexamples to [generalization]. Explain whether they falsify it or merely narrow its scope.

## 6. Boundary conditions

> Identify where [rule or recommendation] works, where it fails, and what conditions separate the two.

## 7. Competing hypotheses

> Generate three plausible explanations for [observation]. For each, specify a discriminating test and expected evidence.

## 8. Scope creep

> Review [requirements] for unstated additions, ambiguous scope, and dependencies. Propose a minimal agreed scope.

## 9. Definition audit

> Find vague or inconsistent terms in [document]. Propose operational definitions that can be tested.

## 10. Argument map

> Map [argument] into claims, supporting evidence, objections, and unresolved questions. Do not assume every claim is true.
