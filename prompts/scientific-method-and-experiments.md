# Scientific Method And Experiments

## 1. Testable hypothesis

> Convert [idea] into a falsifiable hypothesis with measurable outcomes, a time horizon, and explicit disconfirmation criteria.

## 2. Experimental controls

> Audit [experiment] for control group quality, randomization, contamination, carryover effects, and instrumentation differences.

## 3. Sample size reality

> Review [experiment design] for power, minimum detectable effect, variance assumptions, and whether the planned sample supports the claimed conclusion.

## 4. P-hacking signals

> Inspect [analysis plan] for multiple comparisons, selective stopping, post-hoc segmentation, and undisclosed hypothesis changes.

## 5. Replication protocol

> Turn [finding] into a reproducible protocol specifying inputs, procedure, environment, measurements, and expected failure cases.

## 6. Measurement validity

> Determine whether [metric] measures [construct] or merely a convenient proxy. Identify construct validity threats and alternatives.

## 7. External validity

> Assess whether [result] generalizes beyond the tested population, geography, device, period, and context.

## 8. Negative results

> Interpret [non-significant result] without treating lack of evidence as proof of no effect. Identify informative confidence bounds.

## 9. Mechanism versus outcome

> Distinguish observed outcomes from the proposed mechanism in [study]. Propose a test of the mechanism.

## 10. Research integrity

> Check [research report] for missing exclusions, selective reporting, data leakage, undeclared assumptions, and reproducibility gaps.
